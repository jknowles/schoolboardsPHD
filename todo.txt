It is absolutely fine in my book to focus just on Wisconsin. However, be wary about making statements that sound like you are generalizing beyond WI. For example—in the following, I would make it clear you are talking about elections specifically in WI: “In Table 2.6 we see that school board elections are contested more often than the literature from Chapter 1 would lead us to believe. In fact, school board races are about as contested as partisan primaries for Congress.”
 
With respect to chapter 3-- you state at the start of the chapter that the role of school board office as a pipeline to higher office has been overstated. But then on p 5 of that chapter you justify this study by saying that what happens at the school board level has implications for higher office. That seems contradictory to me.
 
After getting several pages into Chapter 3, I asked myself whether this chapter is really about why candidates run. You’re thoroughly describing a variety of characteristics about the districts, e.g., level of competitiveness, etc., but are these really things we expect to propel people to run for a school board? I would think it would be much more issue-based, or more specific to the local context. Now that I’ve read the whole chapter, I see that your results point to that conclusion. I’m not sure what to make of that. I think it might be good to acknowledge at the start of the chapter that people likely run for office for issue-based reasons.
