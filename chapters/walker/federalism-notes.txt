Notes on Federalism

Walker 1995—Explication of types of federalism—lists 20+ types theorized in the literature, discordant and numerous
 
Anton 1989—Federalim research is empirically strong, but theoretically weak at explaining mechanisms
 
 
Grodzins 1966—Marble cake metaphor builds on the dual layer metaphor previously
•         Several actors at different levels entwined intricately
 
Elazar 1984 and 1991—Elaboration on cooperative federalism where there are multiple centers of power
•         Cooperation work is precise, but it does not yield predictions
 
 
Elazar 1991—Argues against pervasive simplistic assumptions, cannot occur/be simplified to labels of dual or cooperative federalism
 
 
Principal Agent Models of State Federal Relations
•         Chubb 1985; Hedge, Scicchitano, Metz 1991; Hedge and Scicchitano 1994; Hill and Weissort 1995; Wood 1992, 1991; Nicholson-Crotty 2004)
•         * Policymakers in DC are Principals, distribute funds to state/local governments (agents)
•         Debatable that states are merely agents, especially in areas where federal government has low responsibility or lack of federal funds
 
 
Berkman 1993—State Roots Model—Tax policy emerged 1978-1986 as a result of Congress members experience in economic development in the home state
•         Affected attitudes and party coalitions in Congress
•         Subnational change drives this
 
 
Chubb 985 and Fester and Kettl 1996—interedependence across policy areas of different levels of governments
 
Growth in state capabilities—professional and policy-oriented—(Anton 1989, Posner 1998, Beamer 1999, Gormley 2001)
 
•         Combats centralization arguments during the same period (Nathan 1990, Walker 1995, Conlon 1998)
 
Derthick 2001—Characterize the literature as one of blaming federalism for frustrating policy development (Anton 1989)
 
 
Schattschneider 1935—New Policies create new policies—positive feedback loops in policymaking
 
 
Karch 2007—Defense of states as laboratories of diffusion/innovation
